## Summary

Wolf Link's charged area attack commits to a targeted enemy when the attack is released. If Wolf Link and the target are separated before that release, the attack can carry him rapidly back toward the enemy. A superjump arranges the target line so this lunge crosses a gap, climbs a ledge, or passes over collision that normal movement cannot clear.

The technique does not create an arbitrary free jump. Its direction and endpoint are constrained by the selected enemy, so enemy behavior is part of every setup.

## Setup

1. Bring a targetable enemy to the launch area. The Gale Boomerang is often useful for moving or stunning it without killing it.
2. Transform into Wolf Link and begin the charged area attack while locked onto that enemy.
3. Create distance without losing the target. The exact movement depends on the room and enemy.
4. Place the enemy-to-Link line through the desired ledge or obstacle.
5. Release the charge. Wolf Link lunges along that line toward the target.

Keese are especially useful because they can be moved away from their resting point and later try to return to it, supplying distance after the charge has begun. Puppets and other grounded enemies can offer more stable height but usually require stricter positioning.

## Failure Modes

- Releasing before enough separation produces an ordinary area attack with little travel.
- Losing target lock cancels the useful lunge.
- Killing, stunning, or moving the enemy at the wrong time changes the endpoint.
- Collision along the target line can stop Wolf Link even when the enemy remains selected.
- Excess horizontal speed can carry Wolf Link past a narrow landing; some setups need a dig, attack, or transformation immediately after landing to arrest it.

## Applications

Superjumps are used or demonstrated in routes for [Early Master Sword](/posts/early-master-sword), [Death Sword Skip](/posts/arbiters-grounds-death-sword-skip), Sacred Grove, Snowpeak, and [Palace of Twilight's west wing](/posts/palace-of-twilight-west-wing-superjump). Each application is a separate enemy-positioning problem rather than a universal input sequence.
