## Summary

The north exit of South Faron is covered by a Midna dialogue trigger. Approaching it normally stops Link from leaving until the relevant Faron progression is complete. Faron Escape crosses the trigger without activating it, then reaches the Hyrule Field loading zone on the far side.

The standard route uses two [Long Jump Attacks](/posts/long-jump-attack-lja): one to clear the dialogue volume and a second, made from the same Gale Boomerang throw, to enter the loading zone. The first jump is the precise one. Link's launch position determines whether he clears the trigger; merely landing beyond the visible fence is not enough.

## LJA Route

1. Stand near the left side of the exit with Link's left foot just in front of the two dark marks in the ground texture.
2. Aim the Gale Boomerang toward the far-left side of the exit. Avoid locking onto the fence itself.
3. Release a jump attack while the boomerang supplies the elevated target position. The jump must pass completely over the Midna trigger.
4. Preserve the same throw and perform a second LJA toward the Hyrule Field loading zone.

If Midna speaks, the first launch was too short or crossed the edge of the trigger. If Link clears the trigger but misses the load, the second jump can be adjusted independently.

## Wii Displacement Route

Wii can instead use the blue rupee near Coro's upper entrance for a [displacement clip](/posts/displacement-clipping). Link is pushed out of bounds, returns to the roof, crosses the invisible boundary above the normal path, and approaches the field load from outside the dialogue volume.

This route depends on the blue-rupee first-pickup message still being available. Collecting the rupee during a failed attempt consumes that setup until the text state is restored; a failure that leaves the rupee uncollected can be retried by reloading through Coro's house.

## Epona Routes

Before Talo's rescue on Ordon Day 2, the closed gate itself can also be clipped with Epona. A jump attack can use Epona's collision to force Link into the gate, after which a correctly angled roll bonks him through the center. GameCube also has an Epona backflip variant related to [Epona Gate Clip](/posts/gate-clip-epona-gate-clip).

These methods are progression-state dependent: once that version of the gate is gone, the setup is gone with it.

## Route Impact

Faron Escape is an exit skip, not a completion flag. It lets the route reach Hyrule Field while the skipped Faron objectives remain incomplete, so later entrances and transformations still have to be evaluated against that unusual story state.
